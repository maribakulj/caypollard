# Structure- and composition-based image retrieval, general vision and art history (state: 17 Oct 2026)

Verification convention: "vérifié" = I opened the page (WebFetch) or read it in a search result with the URL given; "non vérifié" = secondary source only or not opened. Numbers are the authors' self-reported figures. Fetches were done through a small summarising model, so verbatim table values deserve a second look before quotation.

## 1. Scene-graph image retrieval in general vision, and gains over pixel/CLIP embeddings

### Takeaway
Scene-graph retrieval started with Johnson et al. (CVPR 2015) as CRF grounding of a query graph. Since 2023 it uses learned graph encoders. Reported gains over CLIP exist but are modest and benchmark-specific (about +7 to +11 points on PSG and Visual Genome). Entities carry most of the signal. I found no head-to-head benchmark against DINO, and none on art.

### Cited Findings
- Johnson, Krishna, Stark, Li, Shamma, Bernstein, Fei-Fei, "Image Retrieval Using Scene Graphs", CVPR 2015, pp. 3668-3678. A CRF scores how well the possible groundings of a query scene graph match a test image. 5,000 human-annotated scene graphs. It beats object-only and low-level-feature retrieval (vérifié, abstract only; no numbers retrieved) — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2015/html/Johnson_Image_Retrieval_Using_2015_CVPR_paper.html); [Stanford HCI](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/paper.php?id=299)
- SPAN (graph Transformer + image Transformer, contrastive, scene graph to image). Visual Genome, R-Precision, location-free queries, CLIP ViT-B/32 given the triplets as text: CLIP 79.8 / 57.8 / 47.2 at K=10/50/100. SPAN full 86.7 / 67.9 / 58.0. SPAN with node information only 78.9 / 56.6 / 46.4, so about CLIP level. Edges only: 12.1 / 3.4 / 1.8. Location-bounded SPAN: 92.1 / 78.9 / 71.0. Reading by the authors: entities drive retrieval, relations add on top of them. R-Precision is partly defined through SPAN's own similarity, so the metric is circular to some extent. arXiv 2304.00590, v1 April 2023, v2 May 2024 (vérifié via ar5iv summary) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.00590)
- PRISm (Georgoulopoulos, Chaidos, Dimitriou, Stamou, NTUA, arXiv 2512.18407, 20 Dec 2025): image-to-image retrieval, importance-based pruning of triplets plus an edge-aware GNN fused with global visual features. On Panoptic Scene Graph: NDCG@1 88.54 vs CLIP 81.28; MAP@1 91.62 vs 78.96; MRR 95.09 vs 86.90. Other graph methods: Hi-SIGIR 81.24, IRSGS 71.39, SCENIR 51.41 NDCG@1. On Flickr30k with predicted graphs (PSGTR) and BLIP captions: PRISm 60.01 vs CLIP 52.47 NDCG@1, but MAP@1 61.25 vs 60.59 and MRR 74.35 vs 72.59, so the margin nearly vanishes with noisy predicted graphs. Preprint, self-reported (vérifié; the source table was garbled and the summariser realigned the columns) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18407v1)
- Kim and Ko, Sensors 2025 (25(11):3252): sentences and images converted to semantic and scene graphs, GNN with contrastive learning and hard-negative mining. Visual Genome nDCG@50 reported at 0.745. No CLIP baseline seen (non vérifié, search snippet) — [PMC12158163](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12158163/)
- Structure-CLIP (AAAI 2024) uses scene-graph knowledge to build semantic hard negatives and a knowledge-enhanced encoder. Reported gains of 12.5 and 4.1 points on VG-Attribution and VG-Relation over the previous SOTA (vérifié, summary of the paper). Rationale: CLIP does not separate "an astronaut rides a horse" from "a horse rides an astronaut". It is a fix to CLIP training, not a retrieval-by-graph system — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/28017); [arXiv 2305.06152](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.06152)
- Related: SGVL, Herzig et al., arXiv 2305.06343, scene-graph hard negatives for VLMs (vérifié by snippet) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.06343). ScenarioCLIP (arXiv 2511.20274, Nov 2025) and Tigr100k (compositional-retrieval benchmark, non vérifié, abstract-level only) — [ScenarioCLIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20274v1); [IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/keywords/compositional-retrieval)

### Inferences
- Gains of graph-based encoders over CLIP are real on VG/PSG, where the query graph is gold, and shrink when graphs are predicted (PRISm on Flickr30k). For art the graph would have to be predicted by a VLM on out-of-domain images, so expect the small-margin regime at best.
- SPAN's ablation (nodes ≈ CLIP, edges alone useless, nodes plus edges better) suggests relations help only as a complement to entity content.

### Gaps
- No Johnson 2015 numbers retrieved. No benchmark found comparing scene-graph retrieval with DINO/DINOv2. No 2023-26 paper found that builds scene graphs with a VLM (GPT-4V, Gemini, Qwen-VL) and compares them to CLIP for retrieval on a shared protocol; the search tool returned none. Not exhaustive.

## 2. Art: motif and copy discovery, composition analysis, pose, cross-depiction, same-subject retrieval

### Takeaway
Copy and motif discovery in art is solved mostly by appearance features adapted to the collection, plus geometric or cycle-consistency verification. This is spatial structure of local matches, not semantic relational structure. Explicit composition graphs for art appeared in 2026 (CompoVista, Chinese paintings, no retrieval numbers). I found no "same subject across media" benchmark for iconography.

### Cited Findings
- Shen, Efros, Aubry, CVPR 2019 (arXiv 1903.02678). Self-supervised fine-tuning, with spatial consistency of neighbouring feature matches as the signal, followed by geometric verification. Brueghel dataset: 273 near-duplicate details in 1,587 artworks. One-shot detection mAP (IoU>0.3), cosine similarity: ImageNet 58.0, Context Prediction 58.8, theirs 75.3. With discovery score: 54.8, 64.29, 76.4. SIFT Video Google baseline 21.53. LTLL accuracy: ResNet18 with discovery 80.9, theirs 88.5. Oxford5K mAP 85.0 to 85.7 (vérifié via ar5iv, summarised) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.02678); [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Shen_Discovering_Visual_Patterns_in_Art_Collections_With_Spatially-Consistent_Feature_Learning_CVPR_2019_paper.html)
- Kaoua, Shen, Durr, Lazaris, Picard, Aubry, "Image Collation: Matching illustrations in manuscripts", ICDAR 2021 (arXiv 2108.08109). Six manuscripts, over 2,000 illustrations, about 1,200 correspondences. De Materia Medica accuracy D1-D2 / D1-D3 / D2-D3: raw ResNet50 35.4 / 46.1 / 34.1; Shen 2019 56.3 / 71.3 / 51.7; their transformation-based similarity 61.7 / 77.7 / 60.1; with max normalisation 70.5 / 85.3 / 69.0; with 2-cycle propagation 82.5 / 88.5 / 81.7. 3-cycle matches are 97-99 % accurate. Physiologus is nearly solved (98-100 %). Gain comes from matching-graph cycle consistency across manuscripts, not from scene content (vérifié via ar5iv) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2108.08109); [project](https://imagine.enpc.fr/~shenx/ImageCollation)
- AIKON, two 2025 HAL preprints: "AIKON: A Collaborative Research Environment for the Visual Study of Historical Corpora" (Guilbaud, Husson, Lazaris et al., hal-05249619) and "AIKON: A Modular Computer Vision Platform for Historical Corpora" (Albouy, Norindr et al., hal-05248250). Web platform with region extraction, similarity search and vectorisation, built by the DISCOVER, EIDA and VHS projects. Historians validate algorithmic output. Pages not opened; no retrieval metrics seen (non vérifié, search snippet only) — [hal-05249619](https://hal.umontpellier.fr/SHMESP/hal-05249619v1); [hal-05248250](https://hal.sorbonne-universite.fr/SYRTE/hal-05248250v1)
- VHS: a 2023 conference paper describes an expert-in-the-loop illustration extraction pipeline, 8k validated illustrations out of 235k extracted from 405k pages (non vérifié, snippet) — [HAL](https://hal-hprints.archives-ouvertes.fr/FED-3/hal-04316644v1). Slides: [VHS IAMAHA](https://imagine.enpc.fr/~baltacis/VHS_IAMAHA_Conference-1.pdf) (not opened).
- Seguin et al., ECCV 2016 workshop, "Visual Link Retrieval in a Database of Paintings": fine-tuning a network improves retrieval of shared visual patterns. Castellano et al. 2020 (arXiv 2003.08476), CNN nearest-neighbour search for visual links plus graph analysis of artist influence (vérifié by snippet) — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/eccv/2016/seguin2016eccv-visual); [arXiv 2003.08476](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.08476)
- CompoVista (Qian et al., arXiv 2607.07105, submitted 8 Jul 2026, revised 29 Jul 2026, cs.HC): composition-graph (CompoGraph) of four layers (entities, relations, voids, context) for Traditional Chinese Paintings. Visual analytics system for building and comparing cohorts of paintings. Query by weighted structure-aware matching (per Emergent Mind summary). Evaluated by 12-participant user study, two case studies, expert interviews. No retrieval accuracy numbers. The domain is Chinese painting, not European iconography (vérifié, abstract) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07105)
- Behrad, Tuytelaars, Wagemans, "Learning visual representations for compositional analysis of artworks and photographs", arXiv 2608.06142, 6 Aug 2026 (v2 14 Aug), ECCV workshops 2026 submission. Compares a perceptual-grouping method (object-centric region decomposition plus a graph attention network over spatial relations) with fine-tuned foundation models. Tasks: composition score/category, compositional image retrieval, saliency. Result as stated in the abstract: the graph method is "competitive" with frozen encoders and interpretable, but fine-tuned large self-supervised models are significantly better when data allow. No numbers on the abstract page; datasets not named there (vérifié, abstract only) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06142)
- Pose and figure configuration: Springstein et al., "Semi-supervised Human Pose Estimation in Art-historical Images" (arXiv 2207.02976, 2022) motivated by retrieval by body constellation. POSE-ID-on (Marsocci and Lastilla, ISPRS IJGI 10(4):257, 2021): 14-point skeletons, Pathosformel clustering. "Automatic Analysis of Human Body Representations in Western Art" (arXiv 2210.08860, 2022). Greek vase pose estimation (ACM JOCCH 2023). No retrieval numbers read; nothing dated 2024-26 found (vérifié by snippet) — [2207.02976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.02976); [IJGI](https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/10/4/257); [2210.08860](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.08860)
- Cross-depiction: Cai, Wu, Hall (ICCV 2015 workshops; arXiv 1505.00110): no tested method performed consistently across photos and artworks. Boulton and Hall (arXiv 1907.12622): a randomly fixed last layer of AlexNet matched state of the art on PACS, texture alone insufficient. Object-category recognition, not subject or composition retrieval (vérifié by snippet) — [arXiv 1505.00110](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.00110); [arXiv 1907.12622](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1907.12622)
- Iconographic scale: the ICONOGRAPHICS project (FAU Erlangen) targets four scenes (Annunciation, Adoration of the Magi, Baptism of Christ, Noli me tangere); P. Bell reports more than 20,000 pictures per iconography for such canonical scenes (non vérifié, snippet) — [DHSS FAU](https://www.dhss.phil.fau.de/2021/02/13/conographics-computational-understanding-of-iconography-and-narration-in-visual-cultural-heritage/)

### Inferences
- The strongest documented art "copy" results (Brueghel, manuscripts) rely on local-feature spatial consistency and cycle consistency of correspondences. That is a form of geometric structure, but the structure is match geometry, not an object-relation graph.
- Wherever composition graphs for art exist (CompoVista, Behrad et al.), the evidence is user studies or a "competitive but not better" result, not retrieval gains.

### Gaps
- Not found: emblem/print layout-based retrieval, a benchmark for the same subject across media, VLM-generated scene graphs on artworks. Not found: the "Discover/Kaoua 2021" tool page, and a paper on the "VHS" retrieval method beyond a 2023 extraction pipeline. Numbers for Springstein, POSE-ID-on and Behrad et al. were not read. Search tool is US-only and shallow, so absence is weak evidence.

## 3. GNNs and knowledge graphs on artworks (knowledge graph vs scene graph)

### Takeaway
ArtGraph, ArtSAGENet and the Bari line use knowledge graphs, whose nodes are artists, styles, genres, tags and artworks, with metadata edges. They never encode what is depicted in the image. They are not scene-graph methods and say nothing about composition.

### Cited Findings
- ArtSAGENet, Efthymiou, Rudinac, Kackovic, Worring, Wijnberg, ACM MM 2021 pp. 3710-3719 (arXiv 2105.08190, v1 17 May 2021, v2 24 May 2025). GNNs plus CNNs; on style classification, artist attribution, creation period and tag prediction GNNs outperform CNN baselines with less training time and labels. Node information is relations between artworks and artists (vérifié, abstract) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08190v2)
- ArtGraph (Bari group, 2021): knowledge graph from WikiArt and DBpedia in Neo4j; embeddings from the graph are injected into a vision model for attribute prediction. KBS 2022 journal version, "Leveraging Knowledge Graphs and Deep Learning for automatic art analysis". 2023: "Recognizing the Style, Genre, and Emotion of a Work of Art Through Visual and Knowledge Graph Embeddings" (ViT plus graph attention, ArtEmis emotions). 2025: GraphCLIP, contrastive over images and the KG, on ArtGraph (over 100,000 artworks, 32 styles, 18 genres) (non vérifié, search summaries) — [arXiv 2105.15028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.15028v2); [uniba KBS 2022](https://ricerca.uniba.it/retrieve/21ef75df-2c9b-4786-9768-97ba307268cf/2022_KBS.pdf); [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11042-025-20699-0)
- ArtSeek (arXiv 2507.21917, July 2025): multimodal in-context reasoning plus late-interaction retrieval for artwork understanding (title seen only, non vérifié) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21917v1)

### Inferences
- Gains of these KG methods come from metadata context (who painted it, school, period), so they cannot answer "which figures stand in what relation".

### Gaps
- "GCNBoost": no source found; I cannot confirm it exists or what it is. Not to be cited without a reference.

## 4. Evidence that relational structure helps retrieve iconographic subjects (Visitation vs Annunciation)

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is missing. I found no experiment on iconographic subject retrieval, and none on Annunciation vs Visitation, comparing relational/graph structure with whole-image embeddings.

### Cited Findings
- General vision: relations help beyond entities only marginally in SPAN (nodes-only equals CLIP, adding edges gives about +10 points at K=100) and gains shrink with predicted graphs (PRISm Flickr30k) — see section 1.
- CLIP is documented to confuse role-reversed relations, and graph-based hard negatives fix part of this (Structure-CLIP, SGVL), which is exactly the kind of failure that would conflate scenes with the same actors but different arrangement — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/28017)
- Art: Behrad et al. (Aug 2026) find a graph-attention composition model competitive but beaten by fine-tuned foundation models; CompoVista (Jul 2026) shows usability only — see section 2.
- Iconography is hard because compositions vary in setting, style and technique (P. Bell, ICONOGRAPHICS; non vérifié, snippet).

### Inferences
- Annunciation (two figures, Gabriel and Mary, often a dove and a lily) and Visitation (two women embracing) share actors count and are separable by pose/gesture/attribute. The plausible mechanism is a figure-pose-plus-attribute graph, but no tested evidence supports a gain over CLIP/DINOv2. This would need a project-specific test.

### Gaps
- No iconography-specific benchmark with numbers. No VLM-scene-graph-for-art comparison. Pose-retrieval metrics unread. Ideally check ICONCLASS-linked datasets, Bell's publications, and ECCV/ICCV VISART 2024-26 proceedings, which I did not reach.
