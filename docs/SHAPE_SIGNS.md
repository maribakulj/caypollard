# The shape signs, as a historian can read them

A vocabulary found by clustering has signs that are numbers, and a number cannot be argued
with. This table gives each sign two things, kept apart on purpose.

**Its form** is read off the regions assigned to it and is true by construction.

**What it accompanies** is read off the Iconclass notations of the pictures carrying it,
ranked by *lift* — how much more often a notation appears with this sign than in the corpus at
large — so that a notation common everywhere cannot rank by being common. This is a statistical
fact about a corpus of 12 479 works, not a definition.

Across 250 signs, a sign's top five notations share 0.0751 of their
resolved Iconclass ancestry against 0.0365 for sets drawn at random from the same
pool — p = 0.0001, d = 0.61. Significantly related, and in absolute terms a
thirteenth of their ancestry. **A sign is a tendency, not a name**, and this table is an
invitation to contest it rather than a claim to have read the picture.

Labels come from a Wikidata entity carrying the notation, which is not a definition of it:
notation 83 resolves to a particular medieval poem rather than to the class of tales. They are
an aid to reading and nothing more.
